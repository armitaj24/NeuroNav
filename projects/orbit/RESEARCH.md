# Orbit: market and discovery notes

Research snapshot: 23 November 2026. This is exploratory research for a student decision-making and mentoring concept, not a market-size study.

## The problem to explore

A student may be weighing a graduate role, a master's degree or time to explore, while also trying to find someone experienced to talk it through with. The founder's experience of finding a Queen Mary mentoring programme full is one useful observation: it suggests a question worth investigating, but one full programme does not establish the size of demand or explain why places filled.

## What exists already

| Product or programme | What it offers | Relevance to Orbit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [QMentoring](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/careers/find-opportunities/skill-building-programmes/qmentoring/) | A structured Queen Mary programme matching eligible students with professional mentors. | A university-led route to human guidance, with its own application and eligibility rules. |
| [Queen Mary alumni network](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/alumni/the-queen-mary-network/students/) | Students can approach alumni for career insight and application advice. | A useful existing route to a human perspective while this concept has no mentors of its own. |
| [ADPList](https://adplist.org/) | Helps people find and book professional mentors; free sessions are available. Its [career tools](https://adplist.org/tools/) also include planning and preparation resources. | Combines mentoring and tools already; Orbit should not claim that combination is unique. |
| [Prospects Career Planner](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/planner) | A career quiz matching skills, motivations and interests to career ideas; results can be discussed with a careers adviser. | A strong UK careers-discovery alternative. Orbit's initial focus could be comparing a specific choice the student already faces. |
| [Miro decision matrices](https://miro.com/templates/decision-matrix/) | Templates for comparing options using criteria and optional weights. | The comparison method already exists; Orbit can test whether a guided, student-centred flow is more approachable. |
| [MyStudyLife](https://mystudylife.com/) | A student planner for classes, tasks, exams and revision. | A planning alternative, though Orbit's decision and mentor preparation focus is different. |

## A possible position to test

For students who feel stuck between jobs, further study and other choices, Orbit could combine a simple decision workspace, a written set of possible paths, an achievable next step and preparation for a real conversation. This is a hypothesis, not an established market gap. The distinctive experience would need to be tested against the services above, including their existing mentoring and career tools.

International students may have additional questions about eligibility and work after study. Immigration rules change and depend on individual circumstances; Orbit should point to [UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/student-advice/working/working-after-studies/) and qualified university or immigration advisers instead of presenting automated legal advice.

## What to test next

1. Speak with 8–12 students in different courses and circumstances. Ask about a recent decision, what they tried, and the point where they felt stuck. Do not lead with the proposed solution.
2. Ask participants to compare two real options with the prototype. Observe whether they can add criteria, understand the ratings and name a useful next step.
3. Ask whether they would share the summary with a mentor, careers adviser or friend, and what information they would leave out.
4. Record completion, points of confusion and how useful the participant found the output. A small usability study can inform the design; it cannot establish market demand on its own.
5. If the concept is promising, test mentor availability, matching quality, privacy expectations and safeguarding before offering actual mentoring.

## Possible development sequence

- **Now:** the interactive comparison, written scenarios and mentor-conversation notes in this prototype.
- **Next:** test with students; refine the wording, accessibility and ease of use; explore a printable decision worksheet.
- **Later, if validated:** optional human mentor matching and carefully scoped AI assistance. Neither is implemented today.

The interface supports reflection and does not predict outcomes or choose on a user's behalf.
